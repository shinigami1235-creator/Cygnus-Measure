Facial Measurement. Photo and marking protocol

Before and after documentation for tightening, lifting, toxin and filler work. Companion sheet to the Facial Measurement program. One caliper measurement per patient.

What the program measures

Two things, from ordinary photos.

Displacement. How far the skin moved between two sessions, in millimeters, and in which direction.

Wrinkles. How much line there is inside a patch of skin. Area covered, total length, how many lines, and how dark they are against the skin next to them.

Both need the same two things to be true. The program has to know how big a pixel is, and it has to find the same spot in both photos. The rest of this sheet is those two jobs.

1. The ruler. Measure the patient once, not every visit

At the first visit, measure one distance on the face with calipers and type it into Patient measurements. The program finds those same two points by itself in every photo after that. There is nothing to draw on the skin.

Turn on two or three and the program takes the median and shows how far apart they are. That disagreement tells you whether the head was turned. Pick a distance outside the treated area, the same rule as the anchors. For brow or eye work, use the nose height instead of an eye span.

The typical numbers above are not a measurement. Adult palpebral fissure runs 27.0 plus or minus 1.8 mm, and inner intercanthal 38.4 plus or minus 3.0 mm. That is 5 to 8 percent between people. Used as a stand in, they leave every absolute millimeter a few percent out. Comparisons between sessions still hold, because both photos use the same number. Caliper the patient once and the absolute figures are right too.

2. The ruler, second option. Ink dot pairs

Still supported, and useful as an independent check on the face measurement. Draw the pairs with a fine surgical marker, then mark them in the program.

Draw them long. The error in a length is your placement error divided by the length. One or two pixels off at each end of a 1 cm pair is already 3 to 4 percent. The same slip on a 4 cm pair is under 1 percent. Use the Custom pair chip and type the real distance.

4. Wrinkles

Regions are outlined from the patient's own face map. Forehead, glabella, crow's feet, bunny lines, infraorbital, nasolabial, marionette. On a close-up there is no face map, so you drag the box yourself. Keep eyelashes, eyebrow and hairline outside the box. The program reads them as lines. Anything that gets in anyway can be brushed off with Erase.

Per region you get area covered, total line length in mm and per square cm, how many lines, apparent width, and contrast against the surrounding skin. The totals count every line in the region. Report those. The per line list underneath is the same lines one by one.

A photo cannot measure depth. It shows how dark a line is. Depth needs a replica, a profilometer or a 3D scan. Published automatic detectors also disagree with hand tracing more often than not. Use these numbers for change in one patient under one lighting setup, and print them next to the 0 to 4 grade you already give.

Wrinkle photos. Four exposures every session

Toxin trials score lines at rest and at full contraction, because a line that only appears on movement barely shows at rest. Take all four.

PhotoTell the patientScores
Front, at restRelax completely. Look straight at the lens. Nasolabial, marionette, infraorbital, and every region at rest
FrownFrown hard. Pull your brows together.Glabellar lines
Brow raiseRaise your eyebrows as high as you can.Forehead lines

These go in the Expression row of the session card. Add a close-up of each region you are scoring and put it in that region's own box under Close-ups. A close-up resolves finer lines than any full face shot, and the program uses it first when it is there.

The program will not use an expression photo to measure displacement. A frown drags the skin several millimeters by itself, and that would come out looking like treatment.

Shoot the close-ups from the same spot every visit. It will still not be exact. After a comparison, Wipe and Side by side show the pair, and Align lets you drag, nudge, resize or turn one of them until they sit on top of each other. That is a picture control. It never touches a measured number.

Lighting for wrinkles

  • A wrinkle shows up because it casts a shadow. Move the lamp and the shadow moves with it. Fix the lamp positions and mark the floor. No software corrects for a lamp that moved.
  • Light slightly off axis shows lines best. A softbox at 30 to 45 degrees. A flat ring light fills the lines in and hides them.
  • Do not switch polarization partway through a study. Cross polarized light removes surface shine and is right for redness and pigment. It also flattens the surface relief that wrinkles are made of. Pick one and keep it.
  • Blot the skin. A shiny highlight next to a line reads as bright skin and inflates contrast.
  • No makeup, no filters, no beauty mode, and no retouching in the phone gallery.
  • Fill the frame with the face. Fine lines need resolution. Aim for at least 5 pixels per millimeter. The program says when a photo is too coarse.
  • Exposure drift is corrected for. Detection runs on contrast relative to the skin beside each line. Lighting angle is not corrected for. Same lamps, same places.

Camera. Same rules every session

  • Same camera, same lens, same zoom, same distance as the baseline. Write the setup on the study sheet.
  • Camera at eye level, horizon level. Use a tripod on a taped floor mark.
  • Lock focus and exposure. No beauty filters. No portrait mode, its edge blur eats the dots.
  • Neutral face for the resting shots. Teeth lightly together, no smile. Expression moves the tissue you are measuring.
  • Hair fully back, ears out. Remove glasses and earrings.
  • Front view every session. Add 45 and true 90 degree views as the study needs. More views resolve more directions of movement.

Ink against anatomy Ink is redrawn. Anatomy is not. A dot drawn today and a dot drawn last month are not the same point, and the program refuses to compare them. Between sessions the face map and the moles are the measurement. A distance measured on the patient once is the ruler.
Wrinkle sensitivity is a study setting Sensitivity is how much darker than the skin something has to be before it counts as a line. It applies to every photo in the study. Set it at the baseline session, write it on the study sheet, and do not change it again.

What the numbers mean, and what they do not

  • Each view is measured in its own image plane. The front view resolves sideways and up and down movement. The 90 degree profiles resolve front to back and up and down. Movement out of the plane reads short. There is no 3D reconstruction.
  • The anchor residual printed with every comparison is how closely the untreated anchors agree. Readings below it are not measurable.
  • A wrong ruler is a pure scale error. Every millimeter moves by the same proportion, and the directions, the alignment and the session to session comparison are untouched. Placement error is not so forgiving, which is why a long measured span beats a short clicked one.
  • Regional figures average an approximate set of points. The per point reading is the exact one.
  • Expect real skin tightening to come out at 1 to 3 mm. At that size the ruler, the control pair and the camera discipline decide whether the reading is usable.
